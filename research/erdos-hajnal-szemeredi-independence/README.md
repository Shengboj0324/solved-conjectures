# Erdős–Hajnal–Szemerédi independence-ratio problem

Erdős, Hajnal and Szemerédi asked for a graph of cardinality and chromatic number $\aleph_1$ in which every finite subset $S$ has an independent subset of size at least $c|S|$, with the same $c>0$ for all $S$. Their 1982 Theorem 2 excludes a particular Specker graph. The near-linear version mentioned in the modern problem entry admits a positive Specker-graph argument in the discussion. The original linear question remains open here. [Original Problem 2, p. 120](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1982-11.pdf) · [Problem 75](https://www.erdosproblems.com/75) · [Tracking issue #836](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/836).

## An obstruction at the continuum

**Theorem.** Suppose a graph $G$ has at most continuum many vertices. If the vector chromatic numbers of its finite subgraphs are bounded, $G$ is countably colorable. Thus every proposed example in the original problem must have arbitrarily large finite vector chromatic number, even though the Hall ratios of all finite subgraphs would be bounded.

In the vector-coloring condition one seeks unit vectors with inner product at most $-1/(K-1)$ on every edge, for a single $K>1$. It suffices to have such a representation on every finite induced subgraph. Indeed consider all real symmetric arrays indexed by the vertex pairs, with entries in $[-1,1]$, in the compact product topology. Positive semidefiniteness on each finite principal submatrix, diagonal entries one and the edge inequalities are closed conditions. Every finite collection holds in a finite representation, so they all hold at one array. Its Gram construction gives unit vectors $u_v$ in a real Hilbert space with the required inequalities. The closed span of these vectors has an orthonormal basis of cardinality at most the continuum.

Write $\epsilon=1/(K-1)$ and approximate each $u_v$ by its projection $z_v$ onto finitely many basis coordinates so that $\|z_v-u_v\|<\epsilon/3$. Choose distinct infinite binary strings for the basis coordinates. For each $v$ choose a finite prefix length that separates the coordinates in $z_v$'s support. Color $v$ by that length and the finite table giving the coefficient sign at each occupied prefix. There are countably many tables. For two vertices with the same table, contributions on all shared basis coordinates have the same sign, giving $\langle z_v,z_w\rangle\ge0$. Cauchy–Schwarz and the norm bounds imply $\langle u_v,u_w\rangle>-2\epsilon/3$. Such vertices cannot be adjacent.

The same conclusion holds for uniformly bounded finite fractional chromatic numbers. A fractional coloring of weight $K$ gives a random independent set in which every vertex appears with probability at least $1/K$. Let $p_v$ be its appearance probability. The centered indicator vectors of adjacent vertices have inner product $-p_v p_w\le-1/K^2$ and norm at most one. Isolated vertices may be treated separately. Normalization can only increase the magnitude of the negative edge bound. The argument above applies. This also follows from linkedness results for measure algebras, whereas the vector argument needs only Gram compactness and finite-coordinate projections. [The σ-linkedness of the measure algebra](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/0C1F8456C5441CEE76B2304FAC4EC541/S0008439500014132a.pdf/the-s-linkedness-of-the-measure-algebra.pdf).

## Arbitrary finite vertex multiplicities

Suppose every finite subgraph $F$ of $G$ has the following property. For any assignment of positive integer multiplicities to its vertices, $G$ contains the graph obtained by replacing each vertex by that many clones and each edge by all edges between its two groups. Containment as an ordinary subgraph suffices. Then $G$ cannot be an example for the original problem.

To see this, let every finite induced subgraph have an independent set of relative size at least $c$. Apply this to the vertices of a copy of a blow-up of $F$. An independent set in the ambient graph projects to independent support in $F$. The sum of multiplicities on that support is at least $c$ times the total multiplicity. This holds also for nonnegative real vertex weights by restriction to the positive support, scaling rational weights and approximation. Finite fractional-coloring duality now gives $\chi_f(F)\le1/c$ for every finite $F$: each feasible dual weighting has weight at most one on every independent set, and hence total weight at most $1/c$. By the theorem a graph of order $\aleph_1$ with this property is countably colorable.

This excludes all graphs on fixed-length increasing tuples of an infinite ordinal in which adjacency requires disjoint coordinate sets and depends only on their joint strict order. For any finite subgraph expand each ground coordinate to a sufficiently large finite interval in the correct order. Assign distinct choices inside its interval to each occurrence in the requested clones. Between formerly adjacent tuples, disjointness and the complete joint order persist, as required. In particular the obstruction applies simultaneously to every disjoint order type and any union of those types. It also applies whenever the edge relation is open in a Hausdorff topology with no isolated points. Finite intersections of product neighborhoods at each endpoint yield disjoint infinite neighborhoods from which to choose the prescribed numbers of clones. These deductions use the original cardinality restriction.

The finite Hall condition alone gives no uniform fractional bound. Dvořák, Ossona de Mendez and Wu constructed finite graphs with bounded Hall ratios and arbitrarily high fractional chromatic numbers. Further work quantifies the possible separation. An unrestricted construction would therefore have to prevent the finite multiplicities needed in the argument above. [Dvořák, Ossona de Mendez and Wu](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.07327) · [Counterexamples to a Conjecture of Harris on Hall Ratio](https://doi.org/10.1137/18M1229420) · [Fractional chromatic number vs. Hall ratio](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16465).

The source check used the original paper, the subsequent discussion, Lambie-Hanson's theorem and its cardinality qualification, and searches for uncountable chromatic number with weighted independence, Hall ratios, fractional coloring and order-type graphs. Lambie-Hanson's general ZFC theorem allows more than $\aleph_1$ vertices, while the strengthened cardinal conclusion in that work uses a set-theoretic hypothesis. [Lambie-Hanson](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.08177).
