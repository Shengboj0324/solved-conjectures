# Blázsik–Lemons one-intersection conjecture

For a finite hypergraph $H$ with edges of size at least two, join two of its edges when they meet in exactly one vertex. Blázsik and Lemons conjecture that if this graph has even chromatic number $k$, then $H$ admits a weak proper $k$-coloring. They proved the cases $k=2,4$. Gyárfás, Riasanovsky and Sherman-Bennett proved the bound for all uniform triple systems when $k\ge2$. The general conjecture remains open here. [Blázsik and Lemons, Conjecture 3.1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2025.114810) · [Preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12118) · [Uniform triples](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.00551) · [Issue #842](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/842).

## Independent transversals that partition all vertices

Let $F$ be a hypergraph with no one-point intersections between different edges. Partition its vertices into arbitrary blocks of the same size $b\ge2$. There is a proper weak $b$-coloring of $F$ using every color exactly once in each block. In particular joining all pairs within the blocks to $F$ still gives a $b$-colorable hypergraph. This covers palette gadgets made of mutually disjoint complete graphs of order $b$, coupled by an arbitrary single edge class with no one-point intersections.

First there exists one independent transversal $T$. Add each entire block as a hyperedge. The one-intersection graph is bipartite because original edges form one independent class and the disjoint blocks form another. Coincident edges can be assigned to either class. Blázsik and Lemons' bipartite theorem yields a proper two-coloring (the edgeless-intersection case also follows by greedy coloring). Select one vertex of the first color in each block. This proves existence of $T$ and works for unequal blocks of size at least two as well.

Start with arbitrary bijective colors in the equal blocks. If color $i$ contains an entire edge $e$, choose $v\in e\setminus T$. Swap the color of $v$ with the color $j$ of the member of $T$ in the same block. The two colors differ because each block has one vertex of each color. The color-$i$ transversal has gained one member of $T$. Every edge newly monochromatic in color $j$ would contain $v$, and all of its other vertices would previously have color $j$. Its intersection with $e$ is exactly $\{v\}$. It cannot equal $e$ since $|e|\ge2$. This contradicts the hypothesis on $F$. Thus no other color acquires a new monochromatic edge.

Continue repairing color $i$ until it is independent. This takes at most one swap per block because the number of its members in $T$ increases at every swap. Repair each remaining bad color in turn. A color once made proper stays proper during every subsequent repair. This partitions all vertices into independent transversals. For even $b$ there is also a short direct proof: pair up the vertices in each block, apply the bipartite theorem to $F$ together with this matching, and refine each of the two colors separately into $b/2$ colors in every block. The swap proof also gives odd block sizes.

A matching containing a pair from every edge of an individual edge class would suffice for the even-$k$ conjecture if available for all $k$ classes. The union of those matchings has maximum degree at most $k$ and is $k$-edge-colorable; Brooks' theorem applies since $K_{k+1}$ has odd order and needs $k+1$ edge colors, and an odd cycle cannot be covered by two matchings. The matching condition can already fail for one uniform edge class. Take the seven complements of the lines of the Fano plane. Every pair of complements meets in two points, while each pair of points belongs to just two complements. A matching has at most three pairs, covering at most six of the seven hyperedges. The independent-transversal theorem therefore applies to examples where the matching argument fails.
