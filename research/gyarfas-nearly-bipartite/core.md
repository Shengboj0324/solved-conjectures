# A projective subgraph preserving the transversal number

Give each edge a label in $\mathbb F_2$, initially one on each edge of an ordinary graph. The label of a path or cycle is the sum of its edge labels. Negative cycles have label one. A signed graph is balanced if all cycles have label zero, equivalently if it admits vertex values whose sum across each edge equals its label. A totally odd $K_4$ subdivision has label one on **each** of its six branch paths. Write $\tau$ for the minimum number of vertices whose removal leaves a balanced graph.

**Proposition.** If a graph has no two vertex-disjoint odd cycles and $\tau=t\ge6$, some subgraph with the same $\tau$ has a signed skeleton admitting an embedding in the real projective plane in which negative cycles are exactly the noncontractible cycles. Each edge of the skeleton expands to a path in the subgraph with its actual parity. In particular a totally odd $K_4$ subdivision in the skeleton would be present in the original graph.

**Degree-two paths.** Replace maximal paths of degree-two internal vertices by single edges with their actual parity. A transversal containing an internal vertex of such a path may replace it by one endpoint without loss. A subdivision of $K_4$ has branch vertices of degree at least three and uses an entire chain whenever it uses its interior. Thus suppression preserves the relevant paths and $\tau$. Trees dangling from the graph have no effect. Components consisting of a single cycle cannot be the component of interest at $t\ge6$. A negative loop in the suppressed graph would itself meet all negative cycles, giving $\tau\le1$. Two parallel edges of opposite label would give $\tau\le2$. A positive loop can be removed, as can one of two parallel edges of the same label, without changing $\tau$. We only discard subgraphs and suppress paths, so any resulting totally odd subdivision still lifts with its actual parities. Work in the unique component containing negative cycles.

**Replacement across at most three boundary vertices.** Suppose two sides meet exactly in $S$, with $1\le |S|\le3$. Assign edges internal to $S$ to the retained side. Suppose the discarded side together with its boundary is balanced, and has a connected component of its interior adjacent to every vertex of $S$. For two or three terminals choose one attaching edge from each terminal to that component, then a minimal tree connecting the attaching edges inside it. This gives a tree in which the terminals are leaves. For one terminal simply drop the dangling side. Keeping just this tree from the discarded side preserves $\tau$.

Indeed, restricting to a subgraph cannot increase $\tau$. For the converse, let $X$ be a transversal in the new graph and fix balanced vertex values off $X$. If $X$ misses the interior of the chosen tree, the surviving terminals have mutually consistent values for the old balanced side. Any deleted terminals were leaves, so the tree still connects the surviving ones. The old side can therefore be restored using its balanced vertex values, shifted by a common bit on its boundary component. If $X$ hits the tree interior, keep its deletions on the retained side. Among the at most three surviving terminals, compare their vertex values with the fixed values on the balanced side. A common shift makes them agree except at most one terminal. Delete that terminal if necessary, paying for it with one discarded internal deletion. The balanced values now extend over the whole original side. Components meeting no terminal can be assigned values separately. This proves the replacement claim.

**Small separations.** Among all the signed skeletons obtained from subgraphs with $\tau=t$, choose one with the fewest branch vertices, then with the fewest edges. The observations above allow it to be taken simple, connected and of minimum degree at least three. If it has a cut vertex, exactly one component after deletion can contain a negative cycle. Every other side including the cut vertex is balanced because it is disjoint from that cycle. Remove a dangling side. If the graph is 2-connected with a two-vertex cut, each component of the complement has both cut vertices as neighbors. One interior contains a negative cycle since $t>2$. Every other component together with the two terminals is balanced, so reduce one such side to a path by the replacement claim. At least one former branch vertex disappears. Both cases contradict minimality.

In the resulting 3-connected skeleton, a nontrivial three-separation has at least two vertices on each side off its boundary $S$. Some component of the complement of $S$ contains a negative cycle since $t>3$. The entire opposite side together with $S$ is balanced. By 3-connectivity, each component there attaches to all three terminals. Replace that entire side by a tree chosen in one component. Its suppression has just one internal branch vertex, so again the branch count decreases. We have proved internal 4-connectivity.

**Projective embedding.** Slilaty’s Theorem 1.2 applies to a connected signed graph with no disjoint negative cycles or balancing vertex. Its exceptions to a projective embedding are a switching-equivalent all-negative $K_5$ with redundant edges, and a 1-, 2- or 3-sum with a balanced piece containing respectively at least 2, 3 or 5 vertices. The first has $\tau\le5$. In a balanced clique sum, $\tau$ is at most the transversal number of the other summand. To see this, take a transversal and balanced vertex values in that summand. The surviving shared vertices lie on a balanced clique with the agreed edge labels, so the values extend over the balanced summand. Deleting the glued clique edges preserves balancedness. Thus the other summand has at least six vertices. The putative sums give a cut vertex, a two-separation, or a three-separation with at least two vertices on each side off the boundary. All have been excluded. Slilaty’s projective alternative gives the claimed embedding. Switching in this classification preserves the signs of cycles and the existence of the embedding. The labels on paths used in the proposition were always their original parities.
