# Subdivisions of Möbius grids

Take a rectangular grid with $h$ rows and $w$ columns, $h,w\ge3$, and join its first row to its last row by the matching that reverses column order. Subdivide the edges into paths of arbitrary positive lengths. This is the family appearing in Gyárfás’s example for sharpness. The following argument treats all lengths, heights and widths.

**Theorem.** Every nearly bipartite graph obtained by subdividing a Möbius grid can be made bipartite by deleting at most five vertices.

Give each grid edge the parity of its replacement path. A negative cycle is one with odd total parity. By Gyárfás’s characterization there are no disjoint negative cycles or totally odd $K_4$ subdivisions. In the natural embedding in the projective plane the ordinary grid squares and the squares crossing the reversing seam bound faces. If one of those squares has negative parity, its four corners form a transversal. Indeed any negative cycle must meet this square, and a cycle using an internal point of a subdivided edge also uses its endpoints. Assume all squares have even parity.

Switching by a vertex bit adds the bits at the two endpoints to an edge label modulo two. In the opened rectangle the facial squares generate its cycle space. Thus there is a choice of bits that switches all ordinary grid edges to zero. The seam squares then imply that the labels on the matching edges are all equal. If this common value is zero, the graph is balanced. Otherwise it has the signature consisting of zeros on the rectangle and ones on the seam, up to the original vertex switches. Removing the first row kills all seam edges. Alternatively remove a middle column. Each surviving ordinary edge stays within one side of that column, and every surviving seam edge joins opposite sides. Giving the two sides different bits balances the graph. Therefore the transversal size is at most $\min(h,w)$.

Suppose $h,w\ge6$. First consider the six-by-six grid with vertex number $6y+x$ for row $y$ and column $x$, indexed from zero. For its canonical seam signature and any switching of its 36 vertices there is a totally odd $K_4$ subdivision. The [path certificate](grid-paths.json) lists 68 subdivisions, each as six paths. For a path between terminals $v_i,v_j$ with canonical parity $a_{ij}$, the switched parity is $a_{ij}+s_i+s_j$ modulo two. Thus the six paths are all odd whenever the four terminal bits have a particular pattern or its complement. Internal vertex bits cancel. The [standalone checker](check_grid.py) verifies each path, its canonical parity, its endpoints, and disjointness of all interiors. It derives both patterns for each subdivision. A Boolean search with unit propagation and exhaustive two-way branching proves that no switching avoids all 136 patterns. This takes 247 search nodes. Reproduce it with `python3 research/gyarfas-nearly-bipartite/check_grid.py`.

For a larger rectangle choose six rows including the first and last, and the six columns consisting of its three leftmost and three rightmost. These columns are closed under reversal. The selected row and column intervals and their six seam edges form a subdivision of the six-by-six grid. In canonical parity every ordinary interval has label zero and each selected seam edge has label one. Switching in the original rectangle changes just the terminal bits of each interval. The certificate therefore supplies a totally odd subdivision in the larger graph as well. This contradicts near bipartiteness and completes the proof.

The path certificate was found by solving for six disjoint paths with prescribed endpoint parities, then keeping a finite collection covering all switchings. The checker needs only the Python standard library. Other projective embeddings in the [finite reduction](finite.md) need not contain this grid. [Gyárfás’s conjecture and example, §3.5](https://users.renyi.hu/~gyarfas/Cikkek/ar2erdos.pdf).
