# A finite obstruction for the five-vertex conjecture

**Theorem.** Let $k\ge6$. Suppose a finite simple graph $G$ has no two vertex-disjoint odd cycles and needs at least $k$ vertices deleted to become bipartite. Then a subgraph of $G$ can be shortened along internally disjoint degree-two paths, preserving each path’s parity, to give a simple graph $J$ with at most $5(2k^2-k)$ vertices that still needs at least $k$ deletions. Every totally odd $K_4$ subdivision in $J$ lifts to one in $G$.

In particular, if Gyárfás’s conjecture is false, it has a counterexample on at most **330 vertices**. The theorem does not decide whether a counterexample exists. The finite bound applies uniformly to the transversal threshold; the proof does not enumerate graphs.

## Face-width and hitting sets

Apply the [projective subgraph proposition](core.md). In the projective embedding of its signed skeleton, the negative cycles are exactly the noncontractible cycles. More generally take a connected cellularly embedded graph $A$ in the projective plane with this signing and at least one noncontractible cycle. The minimum number $\tau(A)$ of vertices meeting all its noncontractible cycles equals the face-width, the fewest vertices on a noncontractible simple closed curve meeting $A$ only at vertices. Every noncontractible cycle meets every such curve in the projective plane. This proves $\tau(A)\le\mathrm{fw}(A)$. Esperet and Stehlík also state this equality for nonbipartite quadrangulations. Here we use it for general signed embeddings. [Esperet and Stehlík, §1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.07716).

For the reverse inequality let $X$ be a hitting set. The labels on $A-X$ form a coboundary, so switch at surviving vertices to make every edge disjoint from $X$ positive. Every remaining negative edge has an endpoint in $X$. The duals of the negative edges form a mod-two cycle because the entire boundary walk of each face is contractible and has even label sum. This dual cycle is homologically nonzero since its mod-two intersection with a negative cycle of $A$ is one. For each of its dual edges move the crossing of the primal edge to an endpoint in $X$. In the barycentric subdivision this replaces the path between the two adjacent face centers through the edge midpoint by a homotopic path through the chosen endpoint. The two barycentric triangles at that half-edge supply the homotopy, including when the two face centers agree. The resulting mod-two cycle lies in the radial graph consisting of face centers, vertices of $X$, and their incident face corners. Decompose it into simple cycles. Some component cycle is homologically nonzero. That simple embedded curve meets $A$ only at distinct vertices of $X$, proving $\mathrm{fw}(A)\le |X|$. Switching was used only to find a curve, never to change a branch-path label.

## A small embedded minor

The projective skeleton consequently has face-width at least $k$. Take a surface minor $H$ minimal subject to face-width at least $k$, using edge deletions and contractions in the embedding. All embeddings of positive face-width that arise here are cellular after dropping isolated vertices: a connected embedded subgraph with a one-sided cycle has disk faces in the projective plane, as is seen by cutting along that cycle. Redundant components may be discarded. Its face-width is exactly $k$. Indeed, if it were greater, deleting one edge could reduce the transversal number by at most one: append an endpoint to any transversal in the deletion. Every surviving negative cycle still represents the nonzero homology class, so its transversal number still bounds the face-width from below in the sense $\mathrm{fw}\ge\tau$. This would give a smaller admissible minor.

Randby’s theorem states that a minor-minimal projective embedding of face-width $k$ has exactly $e=2k^2-k$ edges. His minimality criterion is that both deletion and contraction of any edge lower face-width. We have obtained exactly this condition. [Randby, *Minimal embeddings in the projective plane*](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/%28SICI%291097-0118%28199706%2925%3A2%3C153%3A%3AAID-JGT7%3E3.0.CO%3B2-L) · [Exact edge count in Esperet and Stehlík, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.07716).

## Lift before shortening

Represent $H$ by disjoint connected branch sets in the original projective skeleton and one edge per edge of $H$. Retain in each branch set a minimal tree connecting the chosen edge attachments. Let $L$ be the union of these trees and chosen edges. Contracting the trees recovers the embedded $H$. The contractions preserve the homology of cycles: an embedded tree has a disk neighborhood, and a cycle through a branch vertex lifts along the unique tree path between its two attachments. Thus every noncontractible cycle of $H$ lifts to a negative cycle of $L$.

Any transversal of $L$ projects to one of $H$ of at most the same size. Map a selected vertex in a branch tree to its vertex of $H$. If the preimage is written with subdivided inter-tree edges, map any selected vertex internal to such an edge to an endpoint. A negative cycle avoiding the projected set would lift to one avoiding the original transversal. Hence $\tau(L)\ge\tau(H)=k$. An odd $K_4$ minor of a signed graph would generally give weaker path information; here $L$ is an actual subgraph carrying the parities from $G$.

In a tree with $d$ incident external half-edges, attach a separate leaf at each half-edge. After removing unneeded tree tails, all internal vertices have degree at least two and there are at most $d-2$ internal vertices of degree at least three if $d\ge2$. In particular the total number $s$ of branch vertices of $L$ is at most $\sum d=2e$. Suppress its degree-two vertices. Contraction of the branch trees preserves cycle rank, so the suppressed connected graph has at most $s+e$ edges. A negative loop or a negative pair of parallel edges would itself give a transversal of size at most two because $L$ has no disjoint negative cycles. Drop positive loops and retain just one representative of equally labeled parallel edges. These deletions preserve $\tau$. Realize each remaining odd edge by length one and each even edge by length two. The graph is simple and has at most $s+(s+e)\le5e$ vertices. It has no two disjoint odd cycles, and every totally odd subdivision lifts through the parity-preserving paths back to $G$. Applying Gyárfás’s forbidden-subgraph characterization at $k=6$ gives the stated consequence.
