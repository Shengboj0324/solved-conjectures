# Minimal transversals and colorings that survive every extension

Let $`\mathcal H`$ be a family of infinite sets with no two different edges meeting in exactly one vertex. A transversal meets every edge. Any inclusion-minimal transversal $`T`$ gives an ordinary proper coloring by $`T`$ and its complement. Indeed every $`x\in T`$ has a witness $`A_x`$ with $`A_x\cap T=\{x\}`$. If an edge $`B`$ were contained in $`T`$, for $`x\in B`$ its intersection with $`A_x`$ would have size one, and $`B\ne A_x`$ since $`B`$ is infinite.

Consequently there is a positive ZFC case of [Komjáth’s question](https://www.erdosproblems.com/602) on ground sets of **any** size: if some fixed finite $`r\geq2,k\geq1`$ satisfy $`|A_1\cap\cdots\cap A_r|<k`$ for all $`r`$ distinct edges of the countably uniform family, it has property B. This is a direct application of Csernák and Soukup’s Theorem 1.4, which supplies the minimal transversal. The pairwise intersection sizes may be unbounded when $`r\geq3`$. [Csernák and Soukup, *Minimal vertex covers in infinite hypergraphs*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10340)

Suppose a two-coloring has the stronger property that **both** color classes are inclusion-minimal transversals. Then the same fixed coloring is proper in every extension of $`\mathcal H`$ on the same ground set that preserves the no-singleton intersection rule. For a putative new monochromatic edge $`B`$, choose any $`x\in B`$. The witness $`A_x`$ for the class containing $`x`$ would meet $`B`$ exactly at $`x`$.

The witnesses have a forced form. For $`y\in A_x\setminus\{x\}`$ the opposite-color witness $`A_y`$ meets $`A_x`$ at $`y`$ and can only meet it additionally at $`x`$. The two witnesses are distinct since an infinite edge cannot meet both color classes just once. Hence $`x\in A_y`$. If $`A'_x`$ is a second choice of witness for $`x`$, it meets $`A_y`$ at $`x`$, forcing $`y\in A'_x`$. Interchanging the choices yields $`A_x=A'_x`$. Thus every witness is unique, and $`y\in A_x\setminus\{x\}`$ defines a symmetric adjacency relation between opposite colors. The witnesses are exactly the closed neighborhoods $`\{x\}\cup N(x)`$ of this bipartite graph. For an almost-disjoint family its same-side vertices have finite codegrees, each either zero or at least two. Opposite-side closed neighborhoods meet in two points for neighbors and otherwise are disjoint.

Conversely, any bipartite graph with countably infinite degrees and all same-side codegrees finite and different from one yields such an almost-disjoint witness family. In every extension on those vertices, every allowed edge $`B`$ contains both ends of a graph edge: for $`x\in B`$ its intersection with $`\{x\}\cup N(x)`$ cannot be the singleton $`\{x\}`$. This also holds when $`B`$ is itself that neighborhood. For example take the graph of finite subsets of $`\mathbb N`$ with adjacency given by toggling one element. Vertices on the same parity side have codegree two at symmetric-difference distance two and zero at larger distances. The parity coloring splits every edge in every admissible extension of this countable hypergraph.

The two minimal transversals are a sufficient structural certificate. The original problem asks for one proper coloring without any prior certificate.
