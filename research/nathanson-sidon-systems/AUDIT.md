# Scope, priority and mathematical review

## Source and scope

Melvyn B. Nathanson, [*The Bose–Chowla argument for Sidon sets*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jnt.2021.08.005), J. Number Theory 238 (2022), 133–146, §6(i)–(iii), especially the unrestricted multiplicity-one question (iii), [journal pdf pp. 145–146](https://www.theoryofnumbers.com/melnathanson/pdfs/nath2022-198.pdf#page=13), [arXiv v3 §6](https://arxiv.org/html/2104.12711v3#S6). The extremal function of §4 uses a tuple of separate coordinate sets in the same interval, with equal sizes. Section 3 defines an unqualified Sidon system by uniqueness of each ordered tuple, so $F_\varphi$ in §6(ii)–(iii) means the multiplicity-one function. It does not require $A_1=\cdots=A_h$ or that the coefficients have distinct subset sums. A common-set variant with unrestricted coefficients would be false, for example with two identical coefficients. No such variant is asserted.

The paper’s own positional example in §3 already supplies the classical mixed-radix principle when the coefficients are themselves chosen as place values. Here the form is fixed and the sets are chosen to compensate its coefficients. The classical Bose–Chowla theorem also gives an independent elementary reduction for the qualitative claim, below. The contribution is an explicit resolution of the posed fixed-form questions and the stronger quantitative construction, not a claim to have invented positional numeration.

## Independent mathematical checks

- **Independent proof of the qualitative conclusion.** Start with a classical $B_h$-set $B$ in $[0,X]$ of cardinality at least $c_h X^{1/h}$, supplied by Bose–Chowla, and split it into $h$ disjoint equal-sized pieces $B_i$ (discard fewer than $h$ elements). For $L=\operatorname{lcm}(|c_1|,\ldots,|c_h|)$, take $A_i=(L/c_i)B_i$ and translate each into positive integers. Its diameter is at most $(L/|c_i|)X$. The form values are a constant plus $L$ times ordinary sums with one summand from each $B_i$. A classical $B_h$-set recovers the multiset, and disjointness then recovers the coordinate of each element. Choose $X$ a sufficiently small fixed multiple of $N$ to obtain $\gg_\varphi N^{1/h}$ in every coordinate. This gives a separate proof of the exact affirmative answer even with every $A_i$ individually a classical $B_h$-set. It does not give the stronger leading constant.
- **Stronger construction.** Checked the source’s ordered-tuple convention, zero/common divisors and sign normalization, the existence of a prime-coprime coordinate for each CRT modulus factor after normalization, and each stage of the recovery of all input digits. The last coordinate gets the whole leading contribution of the lower digit block, so its normalized leading numerator is $2^J-D$, not $2^J-1-D$. The error is $O(q^{h-1})$ in physical width at each of the top two coordinates. Taking $N$ to infinity with $J$ fixed before improving the dyadic approximation avoids any nonuniform estimates. The upper bound counts occupied lattice sites in the physical interval and is sharp in arity two.
- **Reproducibility.** [Direct Cartesian-product checks](verify.py) passed on 19 unrestricted positional systems, 84 bilinear fixtures and 16 higher-arity balancing fixtures. These include mixed signs and the tuple $(6,10,15)$, whose gcd is one but no pair is coprime, as well as boundary binary assignments. The checker enumerates outputs rather than relying on the proposed decoder. The written proof applies to all forms and does not depend on finite computation. No Lean verification or external peer review has been performed.
