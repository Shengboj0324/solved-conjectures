# Chandran–Di Stefano–Haritha–Thomas–Tuite general-position conjecture

**Historical intermediate note. The full proof is now [on `main`](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/tree/main/results/general-position-nordhaus-gaddum) ([PR #858](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/pull/858)).**

This records the earlier partial progress on the independent **general-position** sum and product statements of [Conjecture 9.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13494v2#S9). The separate independent monophonic product assertion was refuted in [#848](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/pull/848). General position concerns shortest paths, not all induced paths.

Write $\gamma(G)=\chi_{\mathrm{gp},i}(G)$, the minimum number of independent color classes such that no shortest path contains three vertices of one class. Let $\tau(G)$ be the minimum vertex-cover size, $\theta(G)=\chi(\overline G)$ the clique-cover number, and $\nu(G)$ the maximum matching size.

## A constructive vertex-cover bound

**Theorem.** For every finite graph $G$, $\gamma(G)\le\tau(G)+1$.

It is enough to prove the bound for a connected graph of order at least two with any vertex cover $S$: colors may be reused across components without changing general position, and the single-vertex graph is immediate. Put $R=V(G)\setminus S$, an independent set. Every vertex of $R$ has a neighbor in $S$.

Form the auxiliary graph $Q$ whose vertices are the connected components of $G[S]$. Join two of its vertices if some vertex of $R$ has neighbors in both components. Since $G$ is connected and $R$ independent, $Q$ is connected. Choose a rooted depth-first search tree of $Q$. Distinct children of a vertex in an undirected DFS tree are not adjacent in the underlying graph.

We now assemble a rooted tree on $S$. In the root component of $G[S]$, choose a root and an internal DFS tree. Inductively, assume that the internal DFS tree and postorder are decided in a component $P$. For each child component $P'$ in the DFS tree of $Q$, choose the **earliest** vertex $t$ of $P$ in its internal postorder that has a common $R$-neighbor with a vertex $x$ of $P'$. Attach the root $x$ of an internal DFS tree of $P'$ as a child of $t$. Continue down the components. Order all of $S$ by processing descendant components before their ancestors, and vertices within each component in its own internal postorder. In particular every child appears before its parent.

Assign each vertex of $R$ to its earliest neighbor in this order. For each $t\in S$ use one color consisting of the vertices of $R$ assigned to $t$ together with the children of $t$ in the assembled tree. Give the tree root a separate singleton color. This partitions the vertices into at most $|S|+1$ classes. For a fixed color $t$, call the children from $t$'s own component *internal* and the other children *external*. The assigned $R$-vertices and internal children all neighbor $t$. They are mutually nonadjacent: $R$ is independent, internal children are DFS siblings, and assignment to $t$ prevents adjacency to an earlier child. Thus their mutual distances in $G$ are all 2.

The external children come from distinct sibling components in the quotient DFS tree. There is no $Q$-edge between those components, so no path in $G$ of length at most three joins vertices in the two components: such a path would either connect them inside $G[S]$, or use one vertex of $R$ as a link between the two components. Both external children are at distance 2 from $t$, so their distance from one another is exactly 4.

An external child $x$ is at distance exactly 3 from each internal child $y$ and from each assigned vertex $u\in R$. In both cases a walk of length three goes through $t$. A two-step connection to an internal child would have a middle vertex of $R$ linking the two components, so $y$ would have been an earlier feasible attachment vertex than $t$. A two-step connection to $u$ would have a middle vertex of $S$ in the component of $x$, since it neighbors $x$ inside $S$. That component was processed before $t$, contradicting assignment of $u$ to $t$. Adjacency to either type is also excluded, by distinct components or by the same assignment rule respectively.

Consequently each nontrivial distance in a class is 2 within the assigned/internal type, 3 between types, and 4 between distinct external children. Every triangle of these distances satisfies strict triangle inequalities; in particular the only pair distance 4 is between external children and cannot split as $2+2$ through a third member. Thus every color is an independent general-position set.

## Two consequences for Nordhaus–Gaddum

In any independent set all finite pair distances are at least 2. If every connected component of a graph has diameter at most 3, all independent sets are general-position sets. If a connected $G$ has diameter at least 4, its complement has diameter at most 2: every adjacent pair $uv$ of $G$ has a common nonneighbor, since otherwise every vertex is adjacent to one of $u,v$ and all distances in $G$ are at most 3. A disconnected nontrivial graph likewise has a complement of diameter at most 2. Thus we need only bound $\gamma(G)+\chi(\overline G)$ in the orientation for which the complement has that simple distance behavior; if both sides have component diameters at most 3 the ordinary Nordhaus–Gaddum bound already applies.

**Perfect graphs.** In fact, for any $G$ with $\theta(G)=\alpha(G)$ the theorem gives $\gamma(G)+\theta(G)\le n+1$, since $\tau(G)=n-\alpha(G)$. If $G$ is perfect its complement is perfect as well, so the equality holds in either orientation. The independent general-position sum conjecture, and therefore its product conjecture by AM–GM, hold for all perfect graphs. This includes all bipartite graphs and their complements. In particular $\gamma(G)\le\nu(G)+1$ for bipartite graphs by König’s theorem.

**Matching barriers in the complement.** Let $L=\overline G$ and use its Gallai–Edmonds partition $D,A,C$, with $D$ the vertices missed by some maximum matching, $A=N_L(D)\setminus D$, and $C$ the remainder. Suppose $L-A$ has at least two components. Choose a component $Z$ with maximum chromatic number $q_0$. There is a matching in $L$ from $A$ to distinct components of $L[D]$ avoiding $Z$. This is automatic if $Z\subseteq C$. If $Z$ is a $D$-component, take a maximum matching that leaves a vertex of $Z$ unmatched; the Gallai–Edmonds matching description ensures that no edge of that matching joins $A$ to $Z$.

Inside $Z$, the ordinary Nordhaus–Gaddum theorem gives a proper coloring of $G[Z]$ with at most $|Z|+1-q_0$ colors. Each color is general-position in $G$: any two of its vertices have a common neighbor in another component of $L-A$, since all cross-component edges there belong to $G$. Use the matching from $A$ for disjoint independent pairs in $G$, and singletons for all uncolored vertices. This saves at least $q_0-1+|A|$ colors from the singleton coloring, whereas $\chi(L)\le q_0+|A|$. Thus $\gamma(G)+\chi(L)\le n+1$.

If $D\ne\varnothing$ and $L-A$ has just one component, then $C=A=\varnothing$ and $L$ is factor-critical. Indeed the number of $D$-components exceeds $|A|$ by the positive matching deficiency. If $D=\varnothing$ then $L$ has a perfect matching. Either way the residual matching size is $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$. For that case, the elementary pairing bound $\gamma(G)\le n-\nu(L)$ is still sufficient when $\chi(L)\le\nu(L)+1$. In particular both general-position assertions hold whenever the relevant complement has matching deficiency at least two. The barrier argument also works on any induced subgraph of $L$ of the same chromatic number, using singletons for omitted vertices; the constructed pairs and distance-two classes stay valid in the full graph.

## Scope

The partial approach below did not address the high-chromatic, perfect-matching or factor-critical complement case. The [subsequent proof](PROOF.md) instead uses prescribed-root matchings and a nearest-unused ordering, and resolves both general-position clauses unconditionally. The cited literature search found the original statement and the prior, distinct monophonic sum refutation, but no equivalent vertex-cover theorem or general-position resolution. [Tracking issue #851](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/851). No new result is claimed for independent monophonic colorings in this note.
