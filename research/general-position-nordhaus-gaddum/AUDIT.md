# Source, priority and review

**Exact targets.** [Conjecture 9.3 in arXiv v2 §9](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13494v2#S9), [accepted manuscript](https://oro.open.ac.uk/107103/8/107103.pdf), [version of record](https://doi.org/10.26493/1855-3974.3454.a3c), Chandran S. V., Di Stefano, Haritha S., Thomas and Tuite, *Colouring a graph with position sets*. We prove the sum and product upper bounds for the independent **general-position** chromatic number of a finite simple graph and its complement. Their classes are independent, and positions are tested along shortest paths. These are two distinct clauses of the source conjecture, recorded here as one catalogue entry.

**Priority boundary.** The authors report Erskine's computational confirmation through order ten, not a uniform proof. [The general-position survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19385) repeats the sum inequality as an open conjecture. [Peter Graham's 2026 paper](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2611.00862v1) refutes the separate independent monophonic sum inequality. The repository's [separate monophonic product entry](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/tree/main/results/monophonic-product-nordhaus-gaddum) proves the fourth clause false; neither disproof addresses the geodesic inequalities. On November 26, 2026, searches covered the exact author set, paper title, DOI and arXiv identifier, statement and conjecture number, independent general-position Nordhaus–Gaddum sum and product, the survey, MathDB, Hexagon, Principia and trureturing, Google-indexed repositories, and the two project branches and tracking issues. No earlier uniform resolution of either general-position clause was located. The claim is bounded by that search.

**Independent proof audit.** The proof's matching step was checked from Tutte–Berge, including the two equality cases: too many odd components give identical open neighborhoods in the critical graph, or the omitted vertex is universal, contradicting connected complement. The order hypothesis is precisely Gallai's bound for a vertex-critical graph with connected complement, as stated in [Stehlík, Corollary 2](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0095-8956(03)00069-8). For the metric step, a geodesic starting through a reserved root must exit its critical block through an outside vertex; its strict distance advantage puts that vertex earlier in the nearest-unused order even with tied distances. Independence excludes a distance of one, so the one possible third point cannot attain any triangle equality. Pair and singleton classes need no induced-distance assumptions. Complement orientation and disconnected graphs were checked separately. This is a written internal audit, not an outside referee report.

**Independent regression.** The standard-library checker computes exact chromatic numbers and matchings by bitmask dynamic programming, computes original graph distances independently, and checks the resulting partition and all its triples. It runs on all 33,868 labeled graphs through order six and 2,000 seeded graphs up to order twelve. It checks 111,701 possible prescribed roots and 10,789 assembled classes of size at least three. The checks do not prove the arbitrary-order statement; the proof is analytical. No Lean formalization is claimed.
