# Polychromatic copies for polynomial norms in every finite dimension

**Theorem.** Let $`N`$ be a norm on $`\mathbb R^n`$ such that $`P(x)=N(x)^d`$ is a polynomial for some positive integer $`d`$. For every infinite metric configuration $`M`$, there is a coloring of $`\mathbb R^n`$ with countably many colors for which every $`N`$-isometric copy of $`M`$ contains infinitely many points of every color. This includes all $`\ell_{2m}^n`$, simultaneously for arbitrary positive integers $`m,n`$ (the coloring is allowed to depend on the metric and the configuration). The [Frankl–Gehér–Sagdeev–Tóth question](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00454-024-00702-5) asks for every strictly convex norm. The result here uses the polynomial hypothesis.

Fix a sequence of distinct points $`z_i`$ of $`M`$, $`i\in\mathbb N`$, and put $`D_{ij}=d_M(z_i,z_j)^d`$. Let $`K`$ be the real closure inside $`\mathbb R`$ of the countable field generated by the coefficients of $`P`$ and the entries $`D_{ij}`$. The proof uses only elementary linear algebra, algebraic independence and real quantifier elimination (in particular, the Tarski–Seidenberg theorem).

## One subsequence for all real semialgebraic tests

For each finite index tuple $`J`$ let $`u_i^J=(D_{ij}:j\in J)`$. There is a single infinite index subsequence $`L`$ such that for every $`J`$ and every real semialgebraic set $`S`$ of the appropriate dimension, membership $`u_i^J\in S`$ is eventually constant along $`L`$.

To see this, first consider an arbitrary sequence $`v_i`$ in a finite-dimensional Euclidean vector space. If infinitely many are zero, pass to those. Otherwise pass to a subsequence such that $`v_i/\|v_i\|`$ converges to a unit vector $`w`$. The signs of all linear forms nonzero at $`w`$ then stabilize. Project the vectors orthogonally onto $`w^\perp`$ and repeat there, which covers the remaining linear forms and terminates in finite dimension. Apply this to the vectors of all monomials of degree at most $`h`$ evaluated at $`u_i^J`$. It settles the signs of all real polynomials of that degree on the resulting subsequence. Diagonal thinning for the countably many $`(J,h)`$ proves the claim by the finite Boolean description of a semialgebraic set. Importantly, its real coefficients may be chosen after the subsequence.

## Finite determination by distance measurements

Let $`\phi(x)`$ be the vector of all monomials of total degree at most $`d`$ at $`x`$. For any realization $`(y_i)_{i\in L}`$, take a finite tuple of indices $`J\subset L`$ whose lifted vectors span all $`\phi(y_i)`$. Write $`a=(y_j:j\in J)`$. Each $`y_i`$ is the unique real solution $`z`$ of $`P(z-y_j)=D_{ij}`$ for all $`j\in J`$. Indeed the polynomial in $`b`$ given by $`P(z-b)-P(y_i-b)`$ has degree at most $`d`$ and vanishes at all anchors. The spanning property forces it to vanish at $`b=y_i`$, which gives $`P(z-y_i)=P(0)=0`$, hence $`z=y_i`$.

Write $`\Phi(a,u)`$ for the unique solution of those equations with right sides $`u`$ when it exists, and the zero vector otherwise. This is a semialgebraic function over $`K`$, by quantifier elimination. All coordinates of the $`y_i`$ are real algebraic over $`K(a)`$, since the real algebraic closure of $`K(a)`$ is an elementary real closed subfield and the solution is unique.

## The algebraic parameters of the tail stabilize

Choose a transcendence basis $`\mathcal B`$ of $`\mathbb R`$ over $`K`$. The support of a point $`y`$ is the smallest finite $`T\subset\mathcal B`$ for which all coordinates of $`y`$ lie in the real algebraic closure $`K(T)^{\rm rc}`$. It exists since $`\mathbb R`$ is algebraic over $`K(\mathcal B)`$. Uniqueness follows from submodularity of transcendence degree: if a coordinate is algebraic over both $`K(T_1)`$ and $`K(T_2)`$, apply submodularity to $`T_1,T_2`$ each enlarged by the coordinate. Independence of $`T_1\cup T_2`$ implies the coordinate is algebraic over $`K(T_1\cap T_2)`$.

Let $`B=(b_1,\ldots,b_m)`$ list the union of the supports of the finite anchor tuple $`a`$. All the $`y_i`$ have support contained in $`B`$. There is a semialgebraic function $`\alpha`$ over $`K`$ with $`\alpha(B)=a`$. For each coordinate of $`a`$, express it as a specified real root, in increasing order, of a univariate polynomial with coefficients rational in $`B`$ over $`K`$. Use a default outside the domain where that root exists. Fix any subset $`T`$ of these basis elements, hold $`B_T`$ fixed and regard the complementary coordinates $`v`$ as variables. Let $`v_0=B\setminus T`$ in the corresponding order and write

```math
F_u(v)=\Phi(\alpha(B_T,v),u),
```

with coordinates put back in their original order. For every $`u\in K^{|J|}`$, $`F_u(v_0)`$ has all coordinates in $`K(T)^{\rm rc}`$ if and only if $`F_u`$ is constant on a neighborhood of $`v_0`$. In one direction, if the value belongs to that real closed field, its level set is semialgebraic over the field. The tuple $`v_0`$ is algebraically independent over the field, so a semialgebraic set defined over it and containing $`v_0`$ contains a neighborhood of $`v_0`$: all the nonzero polynomial signs in a quantifier-free definition persist nearby. Conversely any open neighborhood contains a tuple over $`K(T)^{\rm rc}`$, at which the function takes its value in that same field. The assertion for empty complement is immediate.

The local-constancy test, with $`u`$ variable, is a single first-order real-field condition with the fixed parameters $`B`$ (there exists a positive radius on which all values equal the central one). It therefore defines a semialgebraic set of $`u`$ with real coefficients. Applying the common-subsequence result gives eventual constancy of the answer for $`u=u_i^J`$. There are only finitely many $`T\subseteq B`$. Thus the **exact support** of $`y_i`$ is a single finite set $`T_*`$ for all sufficiently late $`i\in L`$.

## Countably many tails per support

For each finite $`T\subset\mathcal B`$, the points of exact support $`T`$ form a countable set $`X_T`$, since $`K(T)^{\rm rc}`$ is countable. There are only countably many possible labeled tails of the chosen subsequence entirely in $`X_T`$. Indeed fix the starting index and choose a finite tuple of indices in the tail spanning all its lifts, together with their actual points in $`X_T`$. There are countably many choices of these data, and finite determination above recovers every coordinate of the tail uniquely. Finitely many initial positions play no role.

Color each $`X_T`$ independently so every one of these countably many infinite tails receives every natural color infinitely often. Enumerate tail/color requests with infinite repetition and choose a previously uncolored point at each finite step. Assign leftover points arbitrarily. The exact-support sets partition the whole space. Every isometric copy of $`M`$ contains the chosen indexed subsequence and eventually one of the tails handled in a single $`X_T`$. This proves the theorem.
