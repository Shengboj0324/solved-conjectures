# Source, priority and review

**Exact assertion.** [Colouring a graph with position sets, arXiv v2 §9, Conjecture 9.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13494v2#S9), [accepted manuscript](https://oro.open.ac.uk/107103/8/107103.pdf), [version-of-record DOI](https://doi.org/10.26493/1855-3974.3454.a3c), Chandran S. V., Di Stefano, Haritha S., Thomas and Tuite. For finite simple $G$ of order $n$, the independent monophonic position chromatic numbers of $G$ and its complement supposedly have product at most $((n+1)/2)^2$. The source states separately the sum and product inequalities for general position and monophonic position. Both graphs in our example are connected, so this disproof does not depend on the convention for disconnected graphs.

**Nearest previous result.** [Graham, Hexagon 2611.00862v1, November 23, 2026](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2611.00862v1) ([Zenodo release](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23915320)) disproves the separate monophonic **sum** assertion using the full subdivision of $K_6$. It explicitly leaves the product assertion open. Its colorings give exact numbers 8 and 15, product 120, below the bound 121 on its 21 vertices. We use a different sparse cubic family and prove exact product failure at every admissible prism length. The distinction is substantive: the old sum witness obeys the product bound.

**Search boundary.** On 26 November 2026 searched the exact title, author combinations, cited DOI and arXiv identifier, the terms independent monophonic position coloring / chromatic number / Nordhaus–Gaddum product, exact inequality and conjecture number, subdivision / cubic / prism variations, MathDB, Principia, trureturing, Hexagon, the 2026 general-position survey, and the repository's main/research trees and issue search. The prior sum counterexample was found, but no proof or independent refutation of the product assertion was located. This is a bounded negative finding, not a claim of exhaustive global priority.

**Written proof review.** The [Yu paper, Theorem (2.2)](https://yu.math.gatech.edu/Papers/hajos6.pdf#page=4) reproduces the Watkins–Mesner theorem with exactly three two-vertex-cut obstructions. In a full subdivision of a simple 3-connected graph the only two-vertex cuts leave exactly one subdivision vertex isolated, so the first obstruction vanishes, the common-vertex case is a star, and the disjoint-cuts case is a three-edge cut. The latter was checked separately: two specified endpoint deletions would disconnect the original graph if either required segment of the constructed path failed to exist; avoiding those endpoints rules out the only possible chords. A common cycle is induced because each subdivision vertex has degree two. Cubic edge accounting gives the claimed lower bound, and independent sets of one port per triangle plus complement incidence pairs give matching constructive upper bounds. None of the analytic steps require a machine search.

**Independent exact check.** The finite verifier does not invoke Watkins–Mesner. It constructs the order-45 graph from six prism vertices, checks every marked triple using 322,899 depth-first induced-path states (18,570 terminal orientations recorded), enumerates all 262,144 sets of potential star centers for the lower bound, and explicitly checks the two optimal colorings. Exactly 18 three-vertex sets are not contained in an induced path, precisely the 18 stars. The exact counts are 20 and 27; multiplying gives 540, while the conjectured ceiling is 529. A separate initial C++ implementation checked the same triple classification on orders 45, 60 and 75; the standard-library Python checker is the durable independently replayable receipt for order 45. This is an internal proof audit and finite check, not an external or Lean review.

**Scope.** This refutes the full universal product assertion in the monophonic setting by an infinite family, not a search limited to a specific order. It does not re-claim the already refuted sum assertion or make any claim about general-position inequalities or global minimum counterexample order.
