# de Joannis de Verclos’s projective rigidity conjecture

Rémi de Joannis de Verclos conjectured that a planar probability measure giving zero mass to each affine line is determined up to projectivity by its finite order-type distributions whenever its support has Hausdorff dimension greater than one. The authors prove an interior case. Here we give a dimension-one obstruction to indistinguishable points and recover a closed correspondence directly from the order-type laws. [Goaoc, Hubard, de Joannis de Verclos, Sereni and Volec, Conjecture 5 and Theorem 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.02236) · [de Joannis de Verclos’s thesis](https://remi-de-verclos.github.io/manuscript.pdf) · [research issue](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/853)

## The strict secant lemma

If $`D\subset\mathbb R^2`$ has Hausdorff dimension greater than one, then for every distinct $`u,v\in\mathbb R^2`$ some line through two members of $`D`$ puts $`u,v`$ in opposite open half-planes.

An affine change puts $`u=(-1,0)`$ and $`v=(1,0)`$. Discard $`y=0`$ and send $`(x,y)`$ to $`(a,b)=((x+1)/y,(x-1)/y)`$. For two points with coordinates $`(a,b),(a',b')`$, the product of their two orientation determinants with $`u`$ and $`v`$ is $`(yy')^2(a-a')(b-b')`$. If no secant separates, all products are nonnegative. On this transformed set $`|(a+b)-(a'+b')|=|a-a'|+|b-b'|`$, so it embeds isometrically into the real line for the $`\ell_1`$ metric. The coordinate change has smooth inverse $`(x,y)=((a+b)/(a-b),2/(a-b))`$. It is locally bi-Lipschitz on countably many patches away from $`y=0`$. Adding back the discarded line gives dimension at most one. This proves the contrapositive without any regularity assumption on $`D`$.

## From order types to a correspondence

Let $`\mu,\nu`$ be Borel probabilities on the plane that vanish on lines, with identical order-type laws at every finite sample size. Write $`E=\operatorname{supp}\mu`$. Embed the target plane in the unit sphere by $`j(s,t)=(s,t,1)/\|(s,t,1)\|`$. Its closure is the closed upper hemisphere $`H`$. Write $`[x_1x_2x_3]`$ for the signed determinant of the affine homogeneous source points, and $`\det(y_1,y_2,y_3)`$ for the determinant of target unit vectors. There is a closed relation $`R\subset E\times H`$ projecting onto $`E`$, carrying a coupling of $`\mu`$ and $`j_*\nu`$, such that for any three of its pairs

```math
[x_1x_2x_3]\det(y_1,y_2,y_3)\geq0. \tag{1}
```

Indeed, conditionally on a finite unlabeled order type, the labeled triple-sign array is uniform on its relabeling orbit by exchangeability. The infinite arrays from independent samples of the two measures have equal laws by consistency. Draw a common array and couple the sample sequences by their regular conditional distributions given this array. Each marginal sample is still iid. Almost surely both empirical marginals converge weakly, and all sampled triple signs agree. The empirical measures of the paired points are tight by marginal tightness. Let $`\pi`$ be any weak subsequential limit, with the required marginals, and take $`R`$ to be its support in $`E\times H`$. If (1) failed, three open neighborhoods would have positive $`\pi`$-mass and preserve the strict sign disagreement. The empirical measures eventually hit all three, which contradicts equality of the sample arrays. Projection of $`R`$ onto $`E`$ is closed by compactness of $`H`$, and has full $`\mu`$-mass. Hence it equals $`E`$. Points of the equator may belong to $`R`$ and have zero target marginal mass.

If $`\dim_H E>1`$, no $`y\in H`$ can be paired with distinct $`u,v`$. Choose a strict separating secant of $`E`$ by the lemma. Small neighborhoods of its two endpoints preserve separation and have positive $`\mu`$-mass. Take independent pairs from $`\pi`$ with their source coordinates in the respective neighborhoods. Almost surely their target vectors are linearly independent and span a projective line missing $`[y]`$. This follows from line-nullity and independence, since each restricted target law is absolutely continuous with respect to $`j_*\nu`$. The two determinants on the target side of (1) for $`(u,y)`$ and $`(v,y)`$ are then the same nonzero number, while their source determinants have opposite signs. This is impossible.

Fix any compact $`E_0\subset E`$. There is a Borel selector $`f:E_0\to H`$ with $`(x,f(x))\in R`$. To see this, use finitely many closed sets of diameter $`2^{-m}`$ covering $`H`$ at stage $`m`$. Recursively pick the first set whose intersection with all prior choices meets the fiber over $`x`$. For every fixed finite sequence of choices, the nonemptiness test projects a compact set over $`E_0`$, hence is Borel. The nested intersections converge to a single point. Target uniqueness in the previous paragraph means that this selector is injective.

For any great circle $`L\subset H`$ (its intersection with $`H`$ is understood), $`\dim_H f^{-1}(L)\leq1`$. Subdivide the circle into finitely many arcs of length less than $`\pi`$. Two distinct selected images in any one arc then span the plane of $`L`$. Choose two pairs $`(a_1,b_1),(a_2,b_2)`$ from $`\pi`$ with $`a_1\ne a_2`$ and the $`b_i`$ on the same strict side of $`L`$. Such pairs exist since the target marginal vanishes on great circles, the source is atomless and at least one side has positive probability. If the preimage of an arc had dimension greater than one, its secant strictly separating $`a_1,a_2`$ would contradict (1), since both target orientations with this secant have the same nonzero sign. A finite union gives the assertion. In particular the selector is finite in the affine target chart outside a set of dimension at most one.
