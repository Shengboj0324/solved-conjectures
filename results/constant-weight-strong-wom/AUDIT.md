# Source, priority search and verification

## Which problem the construction answers

In their 2015 paper Eyal En Gad, Eitan Yaakobi, Anxiao Jiang and Jehoshua Bruck define constant-weight strong write-once memory coding in Definition 12. For each state of weight $\lfloor w_s n\rfloor$ and each message, the encoder must produce a subset of exactly $\lfloor w_x n\rfloor$ available positions that the decoder maps to that message. Proposition 13 identifies capacity $w_sH_2(w_x/w_s)$ by an existence proof. Immediately afterwards, on printed p. 4216, the authors identify efficient capacity-achieving strong codes as an unknown construction. Their weaker variants supply additional information or impose separate block conditions. The current proof supplies uniform deterministic polynomial-time construction, encoding and decoding for their original strong model. [Journal paper, pp. 4215–4216 and §§IV–V](https://yaakobi.net.technion.ac.il/files/2015/11/16_RM_Rewriting_IT15.pdf) · [Earlier version, Definition 10](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.0972).

## Prior-resolution search, 26 November 2026

The searches included the exact source term, “constant-weight strong WOM,” exact-weight or constant-composition rewriting, constrained stuck-at memories, zero-error dispersers on weight layers, type-A two-write WOM codes, and low-power and constant-power cooling codes. Repository results, research paths and historical issue titles were checked. OpenAlex returned eight citation records for the journal DOI, including two records for the same survey. Semantic Scholar returned five directly citing papers. These indexes are incomplete. None of the retrieved works was found to give the efficient exact-weight strong construction for every state and both arbitrary fixed densities. [OpenAlex record](https://openalex.org/W1890027272) · [Journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2015.2442579) · [Author’s thesis](https://thesis.caltech.edu/8814/).

- Shpilka achieves the multiwrite WOM capacity region. The hashing lemma bounds the weight of stored blocks, and the multiwrite encoder controls earlier writes and its metadata. The original strong-code paper adapts this approach to its separate concatenated model. [Shpilka, Lemma 2.4 and construction](https://www.cs.tau.ac.il/~shpilka/publications/Shpilka-t-WOM.pdf) · [En Gad et al., Definition 23 and Construction 26](https://yaakobi.net.technion.ac.il/files/2015/11/16_RM_Rewriting_IT15.pdf).
- The later polar-code work of En Gad and coauthors controls average cost and error probability in an informed-encoder model. The strong-code problem requires exact weights and zero error on every admissible state. [Polar construction](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.3542).
- Chee, Etzion, Kiah and Vardy study the maximum-weight cooling model and record open efficiency and optimality questions. Their follow-up gives efficient families in several parameter regimes. Chee, Kiah, Vardy and Yaakobi prove the equivalence between their type-A second write and ordinary cooling codes and give explicit bounds with particularly small redundancy through specified forbidden fractions. An ordinary cooling code allows variable output weight. [2018 paper](https://ymchee66.github.io/home/PDF/coolingjournal.pdf) · [Low-power follow-up](https://ymchee66.github.io/home/PDF/lpcoolingjournal.pdf) · [2020 paper, Propositions 1–2](https://ymchee66.github.io/home/PDF/explicitwomjournal.pdf).
- Gabizon and Shaltiel construct invertible zero-error dispersers for bit-fixing sources. Con, Gabrys and Yaakobi treat strong stuck-at codes, including a deterministic construction. In both models the available positions may be assigned freely, without a prescribed weight layer for every output. An unconstrained inverse can return a word outside the required layer. [Gabizon–Shaltiel](https://www.cs.haifa.ac.il/~ronen/online_papers/defects_full.pdf) · [Con–Gabrys–Yaakobi, Theorem 1.5](https://ronicon.bitbucket.io/PapersPDF/strong_stuck_at_codes.pdf).

## Independent mathematical review

A second researcher checked the original definition and open question against the argument, including the exact-arithmetic cost of conditional expectations, rounding and recovery without the initial state, and the uniform entropy estimate for all local weights. Independently of the reference implementation’s end-to-end tests, they enumerated 363 local layers and 92,159 layer/state pairs up to block length eleven. These included 254 checks with at least two colors. The reviewer also searched the original terminology for a prior construction. [Full review and verification receipt](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/805#issuecomment-7190051549) · [Reviewed proof version](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/dc35ef11d5d21e1455f39bfaad958f8f74331123/results/constant-weight-strong-wom/PROOF.md).

## Reproduction

Run `python3 results/constant-weight-strong-wom/verify.py` from the repository root. The reference implementation constructs local tables with exact integer conditional expectations, computes the common message length, and provides the encoder and decoder used in the proof. It checks all 7,252 state–message pairs in three nontrivial examples, the zero-capacity endpoints, and 12 further round trips with 78-bit messages at the theorem’s default logarithmic block length. The latter includes highly concentrated and dispersed available positions. The all-length guarantee follows from [the proof](PROOF.md). [Implementation](verify.py).

The source investigation and proof were developed while studying the subset-coloring formulation of Erdős–Hajnal. That different lower-bound conjecture remains open. [Research issue #796](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/796).
