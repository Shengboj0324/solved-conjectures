# Source, priority and reproducible checks

## The question

Granath, Gyárfás, Hardee, Watson and Wu singled out the sail as the remaining unavoidable four-block configuration whose Ramsey property they could not decide even for two colors. The quantifiers range over every sufficiently large admissible order, every Steiner triple system of that order, and every coloring of its blocks. In 2023 Gyárfás explicitly asked the two-color question as Question 2.8. The proof here establishes that statement with positive-density supersaturation. For three or more colors the arbitrary-system question remains open here. [Original paper, pp. 3 and 9](https://users.renyi.hu/~gyarfas/Cikkek/182_jcd1.pdf) · [Journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcd.21585) · [Gyárfás, §2.4](https://real.mtak.hu/162708/1/ejc40.pdf).

## Prior-resolution search, 26 November 2026

The terminology searched included sail, fan, 3-fan, configuration C15, Ramsey colorings of the blocks of arbitrary Steiner triple systems, monochromatic fans, maximal monochromatic triangles and linear Turán numbers. Searches covered original manuscripts, the later author survey, journal records, arXiv, citation indexes and the repository’s research and publication records. OpenAlex returned five direct citing records for the original paper, fifteen for the first Gyárfás–Sárközy paper, five for Sárközy’s sequel and two for the 2023 survey. Some of these overlap. Semantic Scholar returned three direct citations for the original DOI and one for the survey. The retrieved sources contained no earlier proof of the two-color arbitrary-system statement. Bibliographic coverage is incomplete. [Original OpenAlex record](https://openalex.org/W2755279689) · [First sequel](https://openalex.org/W3107619927) · [Second sequel](https://openalex.org/W4296905093) · [2023 survey](https://openalex.org/W4318016882).

- Gyárfás and Sárközy proved Ramsey results for other configurations, including the wicket. Their four- and five-block classifications explicitly except the sail or configurations containing it. The projective-system Ramsey theorem recalled by Gyárfás concerns a special family of hosts. [Gyárfás–Sárközy, Theorems 1.8–1.10](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.13678) · [Gyárfás’s 2019 presentation](https://oc.g-scop.grenoble-inp.fr/conf/fm2019/images/Andras_Gyarfas.pdf).
- Sárközy’s sequel specifically investigates the sail. Theorem 1.4 forces a sail in one designated color or a wicket in another. Theorem 1.8 allows an extended configuration in the other colors. The paper describes the symmetric Ramsey question as open immediately after Theorem 1.4. [Primary paper, pp. 2–5](https://real.mtak.hu/162638/1/98.pdf).
- Füredi and Gyárfás bound sail-free **partial** systems. Ergemlidze, Győri and Methuku completed the exact linear Turán number. Dense sail-free systems explain why taking a more frequent block color alone cannot prove the claim. The construction in the proof uses incidence identities across the two colors in a complete system. [Füredi–Gyárfás](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.03042) · [Ergemlidze–Győri–Methuku](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.07918).
- The original authors describe uncolored unavoidability and a projective-system Ramsey result. The theorem proved here concerns arbitrary systems of every sufficiently large admissible order under two block colors. [Granath et al.](https://users.renyi.hu/~gyarfas/Cikkek/182_jcd1.pdf).

The older configuration-coloring literature uses the notation $\chi(C,T)$ for the number of block colors needed to avoid a configuration. Grannell and Griggs survey this viewpoint. The disjoint-pair problem in §3 of the 2017 manuscript was already answered under the name *2-parallel chromatic index* by Danziger, Grannell, Griggs and Rosa in 1998. This earlier theorem concerns two disjoint blocks. Its condition permits a sail, whose blocks all intersect, in a single color. Searching the older terminology produced no corresponding sail-coloring result. [Configuration-coloring survey, §5](https://grannell.net/Papers/CONFIGS.pdf) · [Danziger et al., 1998](https://grannell.net/Papers/TWOPAR.pdf).

## Independent mathematical review

A second researcher compared the theorem with Gyárfás’s Question 2.8, independently rederived the spoke identity, the opposite-block parity contradiction, the charging bound and the final inequality. They also checked the count of the seven point roles in a sail and the linear vertex-disjoint packing. They reran the complete verifier. [Review and test receipt](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/813#issuecomment-7191264504) · [Reviewed proof version](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/2182a98ab56502e3e100ef966655acc4981071bd/results/steiner-triple-system-sail/PROOF.md).

## Reproduction

Run `python3 results/steiner-triple-system-sail/verify.py`. The script reconstructs the block covering every pair of points. It counts the four spoke patterns directly, tests the exact imbalance identity, and checks both bounds for the product of the two block counts. It enumerates all 4,224 block colorings of the systems of orders seven and nine. Across those colorings it also verifies all 24,576 disjoint opposite-color block pairs individually. Seven colorings each of a cyclic Bose system of order 87 and a binary projective system of order 127 test the result above its sufficient threshold. The result for all systems follows from the counting proof. [Verifier](verify.py) · [Proof](PROOF.md).
