# Scope, priority and review

## Exact target

Sean Fiscus, Glenn Hurlbert, Eric Myzelev and Travis Pence, [*A New Dominating Set Game on Graphs*, arXiv:2504.03448v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03448), last revised October 30, 2025. In [§5, Conjecture 24](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.03448v2#S5), both path orders exceed one, their product is even, and the authors conjecture a Player 2 win on the Cartesian product. This is the same assertion as Conjecture 23 in [arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.03448v1#S5) and [Hurlbert’s 19-page manuscript, p. 16](https://glennhurlbert.github.io/Papers/NDSGG.pdf#page=16). The 14-vertex ladder meets every hypothesis. The earlier claim about odd grids, the toroidal-grid question, and the separate Kneser problem are not the target and are not resolved here.

The move rule is the shared chosen set described in §1: choosing an unchosen but already dominated vertex is allowed, and the game stops immediately when no undominated vertices remain. This is materially different from both minimizing/maximizing the game domination number and the Maker–Breaker game. It is also different from *Trail Trap*, an edge-traversal game with its own unrelated grid conjecture.

## Mathematical review and reproducible checks

Two routes were checked separately.

1. **Explicit strategy.** [The proof](PROOF.md) uses only graph neighborhoods and paired harmless moves. Confirmed that winning means meeting every closed neighborhood, that neither of the opponent’s first two turns can finish against the specified choices, and that after four moves all listed responses are legal. Checked the exceptional branch with one neutral vertex separately: using the pass makes the two disjoint neighborhoods cover the seven remaining vertices. In the other exception this follows by deleting the two vertices belonging solely to the middle neighborhood. The initial exchange of $A_1$ and $B_1$ is a symmetry of the outstanding incidence data, not a claim of graph automorphism. [Direct certificate replay](verify_strategy.py) checks all 13 second moves and eight labeled reply tables, including both exceptional branches and the endgame conditions.
2. **Independent exact recurrence.** [A separate computation](check_minimax.py) neither imports nor parses the proof or verifier. It labels the graph differently and works with the **union of closed neighborhoods of selected vertices**, not the set of surviving neighborhoods. A selected set $S$ is terminal when that union is all vertices, and is then losing for the next player. For any other $S$, the next player wins if and only if there is an unchosen $x$ such that $S\cup\{x\}$ is losing. Strict supersets are processed first. All $2^{14}=16\,384$ position values were computed; the four winning openings are precisely either row at column two or six, including the opening used in the proof.

These are independent mathematical checks of the claimed outcome, not outside peer review, and no Lean verification was performed. The finite witness refutes the entire universal statement; this is not a claim to classify all even grids or to settle a conjecture restricted to larger minimum side lengths.
